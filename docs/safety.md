# Safety and failure policy

AutoPiOverclock optimizes for recoverability and long-term stability rather than the highest benchmark number.

A candidate fails immediately on any current-boot undervoltage/throttle flag, thermal ceiling event, V3D/DRM fault, kernel panic/internal error/unable-to-handle-kernel report, Oops/BUG/call trace, RCU stall/starvation/timer failure, hung-task warning, USB-device reset, device-offline event, I/O error, buffer error, ext4/btrfs error, nonzero stress process, missing renderer proof, required graphical/explicit-audio/service failure, watchdog-chain failure, unexpected reboot, or permanent-config hash change.

`BOOT_FAILURE` and `STABILITY_FAILURE` define a sweep boundary. A lower passing candidate can still proceed to conservative selection. A safely recovered structured or unstructured harness failure receives five complete-gate retries before it remains `HARNESS_FAILURE`. A strictly proved project network-watchdog reboot instead receives a full same-clock replay without consuming that ordinary harness budget. Exhausted `HARNESS_FAILURE`, a valid protected-hash mismatch, and unproved normal recovery abort because none establishes a clock stability boundary. A primary recovery timeout alone never lowers clocks. A controller-only unavailable-hash result is retryable only when complete protected permanent-normal recovery and a fresh exact hash read prove that no recovery uncertainty remains.

An automatically inferred audio sink that is missing or changed after reboot produces a warning and continues; it does not consume retries or establish a clock boundary. Failure of an explicitly configured `audio_sink_pattern` remains a retryable `HARNESS_FAILURE` and is never a CPU or GPU boundary.

A lost SSH stream is not itself a stability boundary. Every timed gate runs in a unique detached target job with a saved token, specification hash, source boot ID, duration, helper identity, and exact worker path. The target owns the fixed deadline plus a bounded shutdown grace and atomically retains completion size, hash, status, and output. The controller reconnects indefinitely on the same boot and never launches a duplicate. A partial job record caused by SSH transport loss is discarded and fetched again by ownership. If the controller reboots, `resume TARGET` reattaches before attempting normal tryboot recovery. Scalar facts, exact files, validated boot/tryboot state, discovery, and permanent hashes still receive 30 attempts separated by 10 seconds, while safe read-only/idempotent worker gates receive up to five same-boot attempts. A valid protected-hash mismatch, changed/foreign tryboot evidence, and an ambiguous permanent mutation stop on first proof and are never hidden by retry.

A target reboot during stress is called network-watchdog-caused only when one fresh project-owned event binds the exact saved source boot, current boot, configured liveness target, event identity and time, installed config/keeper/service hashes, exact active service, and matching durable prepared, committed, and next-boot-accepted records. The marker remains pending until the next boot and cannot be accepted on the source boot. If the requested reset does not begin inside a bounded interval, its keeper withdraws or invalidates that intent so a later unrelated reboot cannot inherit it. Debian's companion records this evidence but never opens `/dev/watchdog`; systemd remains the hardware owner. Batocera's keeper must additionally prove its committed hardware-watchdog starvation action. Read-only proof is retried while the service finishes startup. Complete proof repeats the same clocks for the full requested duration without consuming ordinary harness retries. Any missing, stale, malformed, mismatched, or contradictory field rejects attribution. One conservative full same-clock replay is allowed after an unattributed reboot. Only a second unattributed reboot of the identical gate may enter ordinary stability and CPU/GPU isolation, and only after complete protected normal recovery. A primary normal-return handshake timeout remains provisional and can never supply network or clock evidence by itself.

A maximum observed pass is never called “rock solid.” Full two-domain automatic tuning isolates the domains in order: CPU search and qualification hold GPU at the protected baseline, then GPU search and qualification hold CPU at its qualified clock. A domain without a clear retained ceiling or explicit maximum sweeps forward; a domain with either clear ceiling performs a complete reverse sweep from that exact ceiling. CPU coarse movement is `max(100, CPU resolution)` MHz and GPU coarse movement is `max(50, GPU resolution)` MHz. Each pass/fail bracket is refined toward its highest actual pass at the independently saved domain resolution, 25 MHz by default and configurable down to 1 MHz. Ambiguous pair history remains conditional pair evidence and does not masquerade as a clear CPU or GPU boundary. The recommended default is two hours for each qualification. A fully recovered `BOOT_FAILURE` or `STABILITY_FAILURE` during qualification lowers only that domain by its resolution and repeats the complete saved qualification duration.

The selected pair then receives one combined CPU/GPU/I/O final validation for the saved duration. An ambiguous full-mode final clock failure saves that pair as an anchor, then tests a CPU-only reduction, a GPU-only reduction, and finally both reductions only while failures remain ambiguous. Each domain uses its own saved resolution. Exact CPU or GPU evidence from any trial immediately selects only that domain. Each changed domain is requalified before a fresh complete final attempt; if all three trials fail ambiguously, the paired reduction becomes the next anchor and isolation repeats. A successful isolated reduction preserves the other domain's clock, avoiding unnecessary performance loss. One-domain mode lowers and requalifies only its selected domain and stops if exact evidence identifies its held domain. If its last permitted reduction reaches the inherited source, it records the rejected boundary, requalifies the selected domain at that exact pair, and starts a fresh complete final; it never lowers below the source or changes the held domain. A hard minimum is likewise never crossed: inability to establish or retain a passing clock at or above it is a clear terminal failure, not permission to widen the range. A safely recovered harness failure retries the exact complete gate five times before unresolved harness uncertainty becomes fatal. A strictly proved network-watchdog reboot repeats the same clocks outside that budget, while an unproved reboot follows the one-replay rule above. Explicit plans retain their configured list-position backoff. Every adjusted pair restarts the full final duration, so any genuine failure near the end invalidates that attempt and receives no time credit. Final clocks are recorded only after every qualification required by the selected mode, one complete accepted endurance and filesystem workload, post-stress health, the configured `final_boots` additional candidate boots and normal recoveries, and a final permanent-config hash check. The approved minimum is three final boot/recovery cycles.

The mutually exclusive `overclock TARGET --cpu-only` and `--gpu-only` modes require an eligible applied AutoPiOverclock result. They sweep and qualify only the selected domain while holding the other at its retained clock after freshly re-proving the live config and tuple. That held clock is a verified baseline and a hard ceiling for the unselected domain in that run. `--cpu-min`/`--gpu-min` set hard floors; `--cpu-max`/`--gpu-max` set inclusive, authoritative reverse-search ceilings; and `--cpu-resolution`/`--gpu-resolution` set whole-MHz refinement and reduction sizes from 1 through 1000, defaulting to 25. Full mode accepts both domains' controls from stock, CPU-only rejects GPU controls, and GPU-only rejects CPU controls. Skipped clocks are never recorded as passes, and no qualification, final-validation, or apply gate is bypassed.

A public `overclock` always creates a fresh run and uses compatible retained failure history by default. Older or missing state schemas are preserved but ignored before current metadata is required. Only strict current-schema automatic-run states for the exact target and compatible hardware/profile, GPU key, stock baseline, and voltage policy can contribute. Metadata-proven unrelated audits and automatic runs with no directly committed failure evidence are ignored before full validation; malformed evidence-bearing current-schema state fails closed. Clear CPU or GPU evidence supplies an advisory ceiling below the lowest proved failure. Ambiguous pairs form a nondominated frontier and retain the interaction evidence needed for combined isolation. A history-derived ceiling makes that domain search downward from the ceiling instead of replaying its known-low ladder.

An explicit domain maximum is stronger than retained advice. It is tested as the exact reverse-search ceiling even when it exceeds a retained domain ceiling; AutoPiOverclock warns prominently instead of silently substituting the lower maximum. This is intentional operator authority, not a history-free claim, and the retained evidence remains visible and available for later isolation. Explicit hard minima retain the opposite authority: no forward search, reverse search, refinement, qualification reduction, or combined isolation may cross them. The controller fails with the limiting domain and required clock when a safe plan cannot stay within those floors. A fresh history-enabled plan rescans the authoritative states and prints the retained CPU/GPU ceilings, requested maxima, selected directions, conflicts, resolutions, evidence summary, and human-readable ledger path. The ledger is created when missing and atomically replaced only when derived content changes; unchanged evidence leaves the file untouched. `--no-history` skips the fresh scan and ledger update; it is not a deletion operation. Reset preserves all source states. Only `resume` continues a saved run; it uses that run's immutable directions, bounds, resolutions, history evidence, and isolation plan without rescanning.

The 3200 MHz CPU and 1200 MHz GPU/V3D limits are sweep ceilings, not safe recommendations or promises that a target can boot them. Full two-domain configuration-free auto refuses to generate candidates unless the active Pi 5 tuple is CPU 2400 MHz, V3D 800 or 960 MHz, and zero voltage delta, and a before/after-hashed audit of the permanent root config finds no explicit clock or voltage control. A one-domain mode instead requires the latest eligible applied result and proves that the live held-domain clock and permanent config still match its retained evidence. Every mode rejects unsupported boost/turbo/fixed-clock controls, unexpected `*_freq`/`*_freq_min` or `over_voltage*` assignments, snapshot changes, and unbound `include` directives. Candidate clocks remain temporary; only a final current-schema result is written permanently after complete validation and fresh apply gates. Tryboot recovery and every normal stability boundary remain mandatory.

Candidate cooling is isolated to the same temporary artifact. The candidate file inherits the complete protected permanent config—preserving unrelated GPU drivers, overlays, display, audio, PCIe, and other boot requirements—and adds only the project-owned clock/cooling block. By default, every candidate and final-validation tryboot sets the Pi 5 first fan threshold to 0C and all four fan speeds to PWM 255. The worker verifies a detected `pwmfan` device at 255 before health/stress and at every stress telemetry interval; a non-max value, malformed reading, or exposed zero-RPM tachometer is `HARNESS_FAILURE`, never a clock boundary. A missing software-visible PWM device is recorded as `not-detected` so passive and externally controlled cooling remain usable without a false 100% claim. `--no-max-fan` is an explicit new-run opt-out that retains the ordinary fan policy and disables only the PWM-255 assertion; temperature, throttle, clock, and workload gates remain unchanged. The selected policy is persisted and cannot change during resume. Normal recovery, permanent apply, and reset retain the pre-existing permanent fan directives unchanged.

Manual `test TARGET --cpu ... --gpu ... --final-hours ...` uses the same candidate ownership, watchdog, maximum-cooling, boot-cycle, health, hash, and normal-recovery gates for one exact clock pair. Preferred `--final-hours` (1–596523) and legacy `--minutes` (1–35791380) are mutually exclusive duration forms, and exactly one is required. Both clocks must be normal-or-higher and at least one must be an overclock. A manual pass remains `VALIDATED=0`, creates no automatic recommendation, and is explicitly ineligible for permanent apply regardless of its requested duration. This prevents a user-selected point from bypassing automatic sweep/refinement, every qualification required by the selected mode, combined final endurance, and final post-stress boot requirements.

The interactive progress display is advisory only. Whole-workflow percentage, ETA, and remaining-test counts re-plan from saved phase/index/boundary evidence and never mark a safety gate complete. Target heartbeats and the saved target start time drive a current-stress countdown that moves every second, including during an SSH interruption; raw telemetry is retained independently and is not invented while disconnected. Progress rendering is disabled for non-TTY output so logs and automation receive ordinary newline-delimited evidence rather than terminal-control updates. TTY rendering selects a content-aware compact layout before the right-edge margin, temporarily disables autowrap, and replaces only the current logical row without a newline or vertical cursor movement before parking at column one. Inside tmux, rendering also accounts for the narrowest attached client. The final worker pipeline consumer remains in the controller shell so display state is not lost through a pipeline subshell. Signal and exit handling clear the current row, restore normal terminal wrapping, and suppress any repaint while mandatory normal recovery is running. Arbitrary client-side reflow or a detached client resized between paints can still preserve an older row; that is cosmetic and does not modify raw logs or saved state.

Public automatic tuning has one accepted combined CPU/GPU/I/O final validation, defaulting to 48 hours. The target, not the SSH session, enforces each timed attempt's fixed duration and bounded shutdown grace. A safely recovered harness failure repeats the complete final gate up to five times at the same clocks. A strictly proved network-watchdog reboot repeats the full gate at the same clocks without consuming that budget. An unattributed reboot gets one conservative same-clock replay before a second identical episode can enter ordinary stability isolation. A proved ambiguous full-mode clock failure enters the anchored isolation sequence above only while evidence remains ambiguous; exact evidence switches immediately to its identified domain. One-domain mode lowers only its selected domain and stops on exact held-domain evidence; its final step may requalify and fully validate the inherited pair but never go below it. Every attempted pair must independently complete the full saved duration from zero. Unresolved `HARNESS_FAILURE` or `RECOVERY_FAILURE` is fatal because neither establishes a safe clock boundary. An eligible completed applied run may start a longer linked final validation only after fresh proof of the exact permanent hash, normal clocks, clear tryboot state, watchdog health, saved graphical/headless mode, and its rollback backup.

## Tryboot file ownership and cleanup

An advanced dry-run records whether `tryboot.txt` already exists and, when present, its hash. Mutating `prepare` and `overclock` require that file to be absent and refuse to overwrite any pre-existing file, regardless of its contents or apparent origin. Project-owned reset evidence can be reconciled through `reset`; unknown evidence must be inspected explicitly.

Each candidate attempt receives a fresh 256-bit random ownership token. Before the worker can create a remote path, atomic controller state records that token, the exact completed-file hash, the header-reservation hash, and a token-specific quarantine path. The worker creates `tryboot.txt` with shell no-clobber semantics, writes through the held file descriptor, and proves that the pathname still names the same completed, token-bound file. A concurrently appearing operator file is never overwritten. Immediately before reboot, the trigger path again verifies the permanent hash and exact completed candidate.

Controller state is flushed before its atomic rename and the committed state file and containing directory are flushed afterward. Run IDs include a random suffix, target worker paths are isolated by run, and tryboot staging/trigger/cleanup, normal reboot, watchdog repair, permanent apply, and rollback share an atomic `/run` lock. Permanent apply rechecks normal-boot/tryboot absence after the explicit command authorization, inside the locked worker immediately before replacement, and again after its verification boot. The advanced standalone `apply` path additionally requires exact typed confirmation.

Target worker storage is treated as transient across every target reboot. One shared controller handshake waits for a changed boot ID, uploads and verifies the run-isolated worker plus detached-job helper again, and only then permits tryboot, clock, watchdog, cleanup, or health evidence to be collected. Debian may therefore use volatile `/tmp` without assuming that any file survives a target reboot; Batocera follows the same handshake even though its run directory is persistent. During a controller-only outage, the target does not reboot and the detached job remains available for exact reattachment.

The controller treats the candidate as possibly live until a separate boot into the untouched permanent configuration proves the tryboot flag clear and the original permanent hash unchanged. Cleanup accepts only the saved completed candidate, exact reservation, or token-header partial file. It uses a no-clobber move to the saved random quarantine path, revalidates ownership after the move, and only then removes it, before the full normal health and watchdog gate. A changed, unowned, ambiguous, or raced file is preserved—at its original or quarantine path—and reported as a recovery failure. An absent live and quarantine path is already clean.

The baseline recovery proof uses the verified stock clocks before any fresh automatic overclock candidate. Stock is the control baseline, not an overclock pass. It must prove the complete tryboot boot, normal recovery, ownership-checked cleanup, and watchdog chain. A failure aborts the run rather than becoming evidence about clock stability.

## Explicit stock reset

`autopioverclock reset TARGET` is the only stock-reset spelling. Reset is a standalone, noninteractive permanent-config operation, not an option on `run`, `resume`, or `recover`. It takes no tuning, saved-run-selection, confirmation, dependency, watchdog-repair, dry-run, duration, domain, clock-bound, history-policy, fan-policy, mode, or redaction flags. The command itself is explicit intent; `--yes` is rejected, and an explicit target is required.

`autopioverclock restore TARGET` is the bounded repair for a permanent boot config changed outside AutoPiOverclock after a successful apply. It accepts only an optional retained `--run-id` plus transport/output options. The source must still be a current-schema, fully validated, applied result with its exact hash-bound proposed-config artifact, matching target profile, boot paths, storage layout, and clear tryboot ownership. Restore shows the exact replacement diff, creates a fresh no-clobber backup, reuses the serialized permanent apply/rollback protocol, and accepts success only after a normal reboot proves the restored hash, clocks, watchdog, and health. It never deletes older evidence.

Reset hashes and audits the root boot config before mutation. A symlink, changing hash, malformed AutoPiOverclock marker block, active `include` directive, or foreign/ambiguous tryboot or quarantine path fails closed. Reset never guesses through included files and never overwrites unknown recovery evidence. Before an accepted replacement it writes and verifies a no-clobber persistent backup; any attributable project tryboot file is backed up before cleanup. Standalone clock/voltage controls remain visible as `# AUTOPIOVERCLOCK-STOCK-DISABLED` comments; the clock directives and markers in one structurally valid managed block are removed while its `[all]` section boundary is retained, and the verified backup keeps the complete original. If the active firmware tryboot flag is set but no live or quarantine path remains, reset claims no ownership and deletes nothing unknown; it prepares the backed-up permanent stock config, forces a normal reboot, and requires the flag to clear. A failed replacement restores the verified backup only while the destination still has a recognized reset hash; unknown concurrent content is preserved. Batocera must restore `/boot` read-only before reporting a result.

Writing a stock-form config is not enough to pass. The controller forces a permanent-config reboot, requires a different boot ID, rechecks the exact expected permanent hash and absent tryboot state, verifies active CPU 2400 MHz, firmware-default V3D 800 or 960 MHz, and zero voltage delta, and checks current throttle/power state plus the active watchdog chain. Reset does not claim the broader display, audio, service, or workload health gates used during tuning. Only its bounded stock-boot sequence may checkpoint `COMPLETE/STOCK_VERIFIED` and emit a reset `PASS` event.

The reset controller uses the existing local per-target and remote mutation locks. It does not stop another controller, issue process-wide kills, or interact with tmux/Byobu; a held lock fails safely. It creates a separate audit run and preserves all earlier local logs, states, summaries, reports, candidate logs, and failure history without deletion or truncation. A later fresh overclock can therefore still avoid proved-bad clocks.

## Active watchdog proof

The recovery gate does not infer readiness from an arbitrary `config.txt` line. It requires all of the following on the running target:

1. a positive EEPROM `BOOT_WATCHDOG_TIMEOUT`;
2. a positive `watchdog.open_timeout` handed to the active kernel command line;
3. a watchdog character device whose live runtime timeout is positive; and
4. a userspace process with an open file descriptor for that device.

The runtime timeout normally comes from the watchdog's sysfs `timeout` attribute. If that attribute exists in any form but is unreadable, malformed, nonpositive, or not a regular file, the proof fails closed. When the attribute is genuinely absent on ARM64 Batocera, the worker may instead bind the discovered owner PID, process start time, command name, file descriptor, and watchdog device identity; duplicate that already-open descriptor with `pidfd_getfd`; and issue only `WDIOC_GETTIMEOUT`. The probe never opens the device by path and issues no write, keepalive, stop, or configuration ioctl. The duplicate is short-lived but adds a bounded reference-count side effect: if the owner closes concurrently, it can briefly defer the driver's final release, and closing the duplicate can then execute the driver's ordinary close behavior. Any identity change, owner exit, permission denial, unsupported syscall, ioctl failure, or race rejects the proof.

Debian additionally requires an active, nonzero systemd runtime-watchdog setting during discovery and after preparation. `prepare` installs a project-owned Debian network-liveness companion without opening the watchdog device; systemd remains the owner. When Batocera lacks a complete chain, `prepare` installs its project-owned service and keeper with hash-bound config changes and verified backups. Each installer preserves an existing managed liveness target. A first install may adopt one configured legacy watchdog ping target or one live IPv4 default gateway only when the source is unambiguous and reachable; no address is hardcoded. Both profiles feed or defer action during a 180-second startup grace, require 180 seconds of continuous loss, suppress an endless loop after three recovery reboots in 30 minutes, and retain those reboot timestamps even after intermittent ping success. They write pending durable evidence before reboot and accept it only after a timely boot change. Foreign project-path content, ambiguous routing, or failed verification stops installation. Every candidate and normal-boot health gate rechecks the four hardware-watchdog conditions above plus the profile's expected network-proof components.

## Resume boundaries

Candidate, qualification, and final-validation operations are checkpointed as explicit substages. Their configured boot loops use range-checked `BOOT_n`/`NORMAL_n` stages: `candidate_boots` is limited to 2-10 and `final_boots` to 3-10. When saved state owns an active detached stress job, `resume` validates and reattaches to that exact job before considering normal tryboot recovery. Otherwise it normalizes any interrupted tryboot, verifies normal recovery, ownership-checks cleanup, and continues completed work. A new `overclock` does not adopt that checkpoint. Every mutating/recovery command remains in read-only SSH monitoring after its ordinary timeout instead of treating that timeout as result evidence; operator interruption leaves the checkpoint resumable. A recovered clock backoff is accepted only after complete normal recovery, empty ownership fields, and independent `BOOT_FAILURE` or `STABILITY_FAILURE` evidence. A proved network-watchdog reboot replays the complete gate at the same clocks outside the ordinary harness counter. A first unattributed reboot also replays the same gate, but a second identical episode can enter stability isolation after full recovery. Current state binds the exact qualification and final durations, selected domain mode, search directions, hard floors, ceilings, per-domain resolutions, history policy, exact qualification identities, target-job ownership, and ordered anchor/isolation history. Resume uses that exact saved plan and never rescans history or accepts replacement search controls. Resume-only `--restart-from` can repeat `current`, `cpu-qualification`, `gpu-qualification`, or `final` only for a full two-domain run; clocks are derived from retained sweep/backoff state. Every adjusted pair starts a fresh full requested-duration final validation. A required move below a hard floor, missing ownership proof, or incomplete normal recovery stops clearly.

`telemetry_interval_seconds` is limited to 1-60 seconds and controls target-side sampling during stress. Every sample rechecks temperature, current/new throttle evidence, requested-clock attainment, kernel faults, and supervised workload state. The detached target supervisor owns a separate boot-monotonic fixed deadline and terminates the worker after a bounded 300-second grace even when the controller is unreachable. Long CPU and GPU tools run in bounded one-hour subprocess segments beneath the uninterrupted shared gate deadline. A clean segment relaunches for the exact remaining time; it does not complete or reset the gate. Workload and Bash supervisor clocks are independent, so only a clean segment exit within 0.1% of that segment's expected duration, bounded to 3-30 seconds, is accepted. Earlier clean exits and every nonzero exit remain failures.

An interrupted preflight, failed preflight, or declined tuning confirmation remains readable through `status`, `summary`, and `report`, including any watchdog-repair hashes. `status` and `summary` use only short read-only SSH observations and do not acquire the controller lock or change boot configuration/project run artifacts. The interrupted preflight is not mutably resumable: resolve the finding and start a new `run`, because partial dependency or watchdog changes must not become a trusted permanent baseline without a new normal-boot preflight.

## Permanent apply

Permanent apply requires current validation-schema evidence, `PASS`/`COMPLETE` state, and proof that the completed endurance seconds exactly match the saved final duration. The recommended policy is 2 hours per qualification and one 48-hour final validation; custom positive whole-hour values up to 596523 are labeled `custom`, and shorter values deliberately trade confidence for time without bypassing any other gate. The upper bound is the largest whole-hour duration below the portable signed 32-bit seconds limit, not a policy cap; multi-week and month-long tests are allowed. A run whose origin is manual `test` is refused before apply regardless of duration or status. Apply persists the old and proposed hashes, deterministic backup path, boot ID, and recovery intent before remote mutation. Resume reconciles a known old hash by fresh normal-boot health, a known proposed hash by fresh normal-boot health or verified rollback, and refuses to overwrite an unknown hash. Rollback passes the persisted proposed hash into the locked worker and verifies that exact destination hash both on entry and immediately before replacing it with the verified backup.

Fresh and interrupted permanent-apply paths share the same fail-closed gate: current state schema, no saved tryboot ownership/quarantine evidence, a readable cleared live tryboot flag, and an absent `tryboot.txt`. The explicit `overclock` command authorizes application of its own fully validated result after displaying and retaining the exact diff; the standalone advanced `apply` command requires exact typed confirmation, and `--yes` cannot bypass it. A config that already matches the proposal still requires a fresh normal boot and health gate before it is recorded as applied.
